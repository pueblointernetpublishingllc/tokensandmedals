Price guide

Our estimates of what pieces are worth, by grade, each with a range. They are guided by a statistical formula built from sales we studied; the sales themselves are not reproduced here.

Our opinion, not a promise

Every figure in this guide is Tokens and Medals' own estimate, guided by a statistical formula we built from sales we studied. It is an opinion of value. It is not an appraisal, not an offer to buy or sell, and not a prediction of what your piece will bring.

Prices at any one venue, whether a major auction, a weekly online sale, a show, a dealer or eBay, can land well above or below these figures. Eye appeal, toning, strike, pedigree, the day of the sale and who happens to be bidding all move prices in ways no formula sees. The range is where we expect about four sales in five to land, so about one sale in five will land outside it.

Do not rely on this guide alone to buy, sell, insure or value a piece for tax or estate purposes. For those, get a qualified appraisal.

How the figures are made

We gathered completed sales from both public and private sources and worked out which things about a piece and a sale actually predict its price. A few things are in by design: grade and date in both guides, metal and the grading service for so-called dollars, and, for Carr pieces, how a piece was sold (auction or fixed price) and how many coins a set or lot holds, because each figure is quoted for a particular holder, kind of sale or number of pieces. Everything else stayed in only if it improved predictions of sales the formula had not seen. Each piece's estimate then blends its own past sales, adjusted to today's terms, with what similar pieces bring.

The test that matters is the next sale. We predicted sales one month at a time, using only the sales that came before each one, and compared the guess with what the piece actually brought. Each guide's page gives its own results and explains what goes into it.

Each guide shows the month its data runs through, and we update the guides as new sales come in.

What you won't find here

  • Any individual sale: no prices realized, no dates, no lot numbers, no listings. We studied those sales; we don't republish them.
  • Unmarked guesses. Pieces with no sales in the records we gathered do appear, but only as formula estimates shown in blue and labelled “formula only”, with a much wider range.
