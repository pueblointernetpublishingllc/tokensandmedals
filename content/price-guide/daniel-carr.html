Our opinion, not a promise

These are Tokens and Medals' own estimates from a statistical formula. They are not appraisals, not offers to buy or sell, and not a prediction of what your piece will bring. Real prices at any venue can land well above or below them; about one sale in five lands outside the range shown. Do not rely on this guide alone to buy, sell, insure or value a piece for tax or estate purposes. Tokens and Medals is not affiliated with Daniel Carr or Moonlight Mint.

Figures in blue, marked “formula only”, are extrapolations. We have no sales of those pieces in the records we gathered, so there is no price data for the piece itself: the figure comes only from what other pieces of the same issue or design, the same catalogue section and the same metal bring, and it is much less certain.

How to read it

  • The bold figure is our estimate as the piece would sell today on eBay at a fixed price. The smaller figures are the range we expect about four sales in five to land in.
  • 70 slab and 69 or lower are for graded pieces (most of the graded Carr pieces in the sales we studied were ANACS slabs); Raw is ungraded. A dash means we have no basis for that grade: for a piece with sales, none of its sales in our records was in that grade; for a piece with no sales in our records, ANACS reports no slabs at that grade for the piece or its issue, so there is nothing to price.
  • Catalogue numbers link to that number in the maker's catalogue (most browsers jump straight to it), fantasy dates link to the maker's write-up of the over-strike, and each section links to the maker's catalogue page and store. Where we matched a catalogue number to a product in the maker's store, a small “store” link beside the number opens that product. Pieces without sales in our records that the formula cannot tell apart, such as the finishes of one issue, share a line that lists all their numbers.
  • Pieces that contain gold or silver follow the metal: the line above the list shows the gold and silver price in use, live from Chainlink's on-chain price feeds when your browser can reach them.
  • Blue figures are formula-only estimates for pieces with no sales in our records. How we make these figures

The formula was built from completed eBay sales of Carr's pieces, each matched to the maker's catalogue or over-strike list by matching rules that were then spot-checked against a random sample of sales. We studied those sales; none of them is reproduced here.

What goes in

  • Grade. A 70 slab brought about {{CARR_70}} more than a raw piece, and a slab of 69 or lower about {{CARR_69}} more.
  • Gold and silver. A piece that contains gold or silver is priced as its metal at today's price plus a premium. The metal content comes from the weight and fineness in the maker's catalogue (where he gives a weight but no fineness, .999, his bullion standard, is assumed); where the catalogue gives no weight, from the weight on his store page for that piece (the low end, where the store gives a range); or, for an over-strike, from the genuine coin it is struck on, such as the 0.7734 oz of silver in a Morgan or Peace dollar. Where neither states a weight, as for many 39mm silver pieces, the figure cannot include the metal and is not floored at it. Platinum is valued at the latest monthly average ({{CARR_PT_SPOT}} an ounce), as there is no live platinum feed. The premium is what the formula learns from sales, each sale's metal valued at the gold or silver price on the day it sold. Beyond the metal, silver pieces carried a premium about {{CARR_SILVER}} times that of base-metal pieces of the same kind.
  • Sets and lots. They are priced by how many coins they hold: a five-coin set brought about {{CARR_LOT5}} times a single piece of the same kind. This one is in by design, because a set is a different thing from a single piece.
  • How it sold. Auctions ran about {{CARR_AUCTION}} below fixed-price sales. This one is in by design, because the guide is priced at fixed price.
  • The date of the sale. Once the metal is separated out, premiums moved about {{CARR_MONTH}} a month on average across the period, and every estimate is brought forward to today.
  • The piece's own sales. Each is adjusted to the grade and date being priced, the middle of them is taken, and the result is blended with what similar pieces bring (the same issue or over-strike design, then the same catalogue section), so one odd result can't set the price on its own.

Why melt plus premium

We tested pricing pieces as metal plus premium against pricing the whole piece directly, on sales the formula had not seen. For pieces with sales of their own it was more accurate, for pieces with metal and without, and it is what the guide uses for them. For pieces with no sales in our records, pricing the whole piece directly tested slightly better, so those figures come from the formula directly and are simply never shown below the value of the metal they contain.

Pieces with no sales in our records

For a piece with no sales in our records, the figure is the formula's value from what other pieces of the same issue or over-strike design bring, the catalogue section, the metal and the grade, with no adjustment from sales of its own. To check it, we went back through every month of sales and predicted each sale that was the first of its piece in our records, using only the months before it: {{CARR_FORMULA_TESTED}} such sales in all. Half landed within {{CARR_FORMULA_MISS}} of the figure, and the range shown caught {{CARR_FORMULA_INSIDE}} of them when tested on pieces it had not been set from. Pieces from issues with no sales in our records did not need a range of their own. The graded columns appear only where ANACS's population report shows slabs at that grade for the piece or its issue. For a piece with no sales in our records that contains silver, the figure never falls below the value of that silver at the live price. Gold pieces with no sales in our records are left out: pricing them as metal plus premium would need first gold sales in our records to test against, and there has been only one. Palladium pieces with no sales in our records are left out too, as there is no palladium price to value their metal at.

What we tested and left out

The grading service, proof finish, the maker's issue price and published mintage, whether a piece is an over-strike or a catalogue issue, a separate price trend for each issue, and ANACS population counts (how many pieces are graded 70, what share of graded pieces reached 70, and whether a piece sits at the top grade with only a few there) were all tried. Once grade, metal and the piece itself were known, none improved the predictions of sales the formula hadn't seen reliably enough to keep, so none is used.

How well it works

We predicted each month's sales using only the months before it. Half of those sales landed within {{CARR_MISS}} of our figure, and {{CARR_NEWEST_INSIDE}} of the most recent months' sales fell inside the range. In the latest months Carr prices came in about {{CARR_SOFT}} under what the earlier months suggested; this edition includes those sales, and the next one will show whether the softening holds.

What isn't listed

Pieces we could not identify with confidence from the sale descriptions, mixed lots, sizes sold together under one name, the individual Lesher merchant imprints (the Lesher line covers them), issues the maker lists as never released or with none struck, and gold and palladium pieces with no sales in our records. A piece missing from the guide is not a sign that it is common or rare.
